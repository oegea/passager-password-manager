Security you can verify

We can't read your data. By design.

No "trust us". Encryption happens in your browser, with your browser's native cryptography, and the code is public.

  1. 1

    A master password only you know

    It is turned into an encryption key with PBKDF2 and 600,000 rounds, which makes guessing it painfully slow. It never leaves your device.

  2. 2

    A personal key pair

    Each person gets an RSA-4096 key pair. The private half is locked with your master key before it is stored anywhere.

  3. 3

    One key per folder

    Every folder has its own AES-256 key. What you save is encrypted with it, on your device, before it is sent.

  4. 4

    Sharing without exposing

    To share a folder, its key is encrypted with your teammate's public key. Only they can open it. The server just passes along a sealed envelope.

What the server stores

Encrypted blobs, the names you give to folders and items, your e-mail, and who a folder is shared with. Never a password, never a key it can use.

What nobody can do

Recover your master password. Not us, not your administrator. If you lose it, your data stays locked — that is the point.

What you can check

All of it. The app, the backend and the tests are open source, with a deliberately short list of dependencies.

The story

Built because nothing else fit.

The world didn't need another password manager. But I needed one that was open source, simple to use, and easy to install and maintain for a team — and I never found it.

So I built Passager around three ideas that still guide every decision: simple for users and for whoever runs it, secure by design, and usable by teams without weakening that security.

— Oriol Egea, creator of Passager

  1. 2022 · The beginning

    An open source vault

    Passager is released under the AGPL, with all the encryption done in the browser from day one.

  2. Independence

    A backend anyone can host

    The first versions leaned on a third-party cloud. It was replaced with a small backend of our own, so every organization keeps its data on its own infrastructure.

  3. Everywhere

    On phones too

    Passager reaches iOS and Android, with biometric unlock, so the vault is always in your pocket.

  4. Today · Passager 2

    Lighter, stronger, for everything

    One installable web app instead of separate native apps. Stronger key derivation, a backend with just two dependencies, new kinds of items, large files and a completely new design.

Good questions

Before you trust it with your secrets.

Is it really free?

Yes. Passager is open source under the AGPL-3.0 license. There are no paid plans, no ads and no tracking.

What happens if I forget my master password?

Nobody can recover it, because nobody else ever has it. That is what keeps your data private. Choose one you will remember and keep a backup of your vault.

Where is my data stored?

In personal mode, only in the browser of your device. In organization mode, encrypted, on the server your organization runs. We do not operate a central service that holds your vault.

Is there an app for my phone?

Passager is a web app you can install. Open it in your browser and choose "Install" or "Add to Home Screen": it gets its own icon, opens full screen, works offline and unlocks with Face ID or your fingerprint.

Can I move my passwords out later?

Always. You can download a complete backup of your vault at any time. Your data is yours.

Has it been audited?

Not by an external firm yet, and we would rather tell you than imply otherwise. What we can offer is full transparency: standard, well-known algorithms, the browser's native cryptography, and every line of code open for review.
